    02 — BUILD YOUR PRESENCE

    Make your work easy to understand

    Make it easy for someone to understand what you do, what you are learning, and how to reach you.

    03 — CONNECT

    Find people, then start a conversation

    Look for genuine overlap, understand the person's work, and send a concise, respectful message.

    Look for relevance, not popularity.Search by role, technology, school, project, event, or community. Read a person's public profile before contacting them.

    Short message guide

    • Use a specific subject or opening line.
    • Say where you found the person or what work connected you.
    • Share one relevant detail about your project or learning.
    • Ask one focused question they can answer briefly.

    Before you send

    • Check the person's preferred contact channel.
    • Personalize with a real detail and proofread names, links, and tone.
    • Do not mass-send identical messages.
    • Make it easy to decline or not respond; follow up once at most.

    Examples to personalize

    Alumnus: Hi [Name], I’m a [field] student at [school]. I read about your work on [specific topic]. Could I ask how you chose your first role in [area]?

    After an event: Hi [Name], I enjoyed your point about [specific idea] at [event]. I’m trying [related project/next step] and found your suggestion useful. Thank you for sharing it.

    Open source: Hello [Name/team], I read [project guide/issue] and tried [steps]. I’m unsure about [specific question]. Is [preferred channel or next step] appropriate?

    Replace every bracket with a true detail and use each example only when it fits.

    05 — PARTICIPATE

    Attend, join, contribute, or build

    Choose a way to take part that fits your interests, time, and current experience.

    Attend / Join

    BEFORE

    Prepare with intent

    • Write a 20-second introduction.
    • Review the event theme and speakers.
    • Pick one or two genuine questions.
    • Set a small goal, such as joining one discussion.
    DURING

    Take part naturally

    • Attend a session or help with a project.
    • Ask a clear question and listen.
    • Introduce yourself when there is a natural opening.
    • Respect personal space and conversation boundaries.
    AFTER

    Continue thoughtfully

    • Send a short note that names the conversation.
    • Share a useful link only if relevant.
    • Record a next action for yourself.
    • Follow up once; let the relationship develop over time.

    Contribute / Build

      Safety & respect

      Only work in repositories where you have permission. Never probe private systems, bypass access controls, or submit changes that violate a project's license or contribution guidelines.

      06 — BUILD RELATIONSHIPS

      Let useful conversations grow

      Keep mentorship thoughtful, follow up with care, and record only the details that help you remember a professional interaction.

      What makes a good mentor?

      Someone who listens, asks useful questions, shares relevant experience, and respects your decisions. A mentor does not need to have every answer.

      How to find and approach one

      Look for a person whose work connects to your interests. Start with one specific question or a short conversation; do not ask for a long-term commitment immediately.

      Make each conversation worthwhile

      Share what you have tried, prepare one or two questions, respect the agreed time, and send a brief thank-you with what you will do next.

      Let the relationship develop

      Follow up when you have acted on their advice. Ask before scheduling another conversation, and accept a pause or a no without pressure.
